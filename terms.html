3. What a proof shows, and what it does not

An Imprint proof shows that a photo and the readings recorded with it were signed by a key inside one phone's secure chip, have not been changed since, and existed no later than the Solana block that sealed their fingerprint. Imprint flags some warning signs (for example a simulated position, an old position fix or a doubtful clock) but does not block them.

A proof does not show that the scene was not staged, who held the phone, or that every reading is true: sensors and positions can be wrong or, with advanced means, manipulated. Imprint is a technical tool, not a legal certification and not a public officer's report. Whoever receives a proof, including a court, decides how much weight to give it.
